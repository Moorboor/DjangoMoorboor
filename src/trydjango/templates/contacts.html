In search of alternative lifes in Central America

What are alternative lifes? To answer that question, we need to know what the difference between a normal life and an alternative would be? Mostly, I think of it as a way of living and the morality that comes with it. What has value and what is worth to pursue? When we know that, then we would have the foundation to act. If we say that we won't define define this morality, then we would still act according a not well defined system of values. For us it is the other way around. We want to have a look on the actions and come up with a morality, which explains as best as possible how the people acted. In central america, I have meet many people with different opinions about what it means to be human and how we should act. Furthermore, people want to have an explanation for their existance. They want to hear that their life is meaningful. In some sense they want answers to the three questions: - Why are we here? - What do we do? - Where do we go? The core beliefs including morality serve as foundation for other beliefs. The core beliefs are so deeply rooted that they may influence the basic processes of cognition, perception and reasoning. It's hard to argue that basic qualities like these are changed by differing core beliefs. It's certainly true that the capabilities are relatively equal in all humans. But how we reason and think is pretty much shaped by the framing and emotional states that influence our capabilities. Is it possible to incorporate all the external factors and their interplay into an internal model that is formed by experiences? And if so then I would propose that this internal model is internalized by the story that answers the prior three questions. The questions that we are asking are in the field of metaethics, moral psychology and cognitive psychology. Before I went to Central America, I pursued many values that I would today identify as transhumanism, a philosophy that pictures technology as a way to improve human life. Additionally, I used to have a form of platonisim, since I was convinced that the ultimate goal was to become a wise person that studies math, physics, computer science, arts and workout to become fullfiled. In my environment everything seemed to work that way. I knew that there are many people that pursue other things in life like money, power, social status. Some people chased other things like social and human values.

That was exactly was I saw. Little corn island grows their own food to feed their people. They were only to a small extent dependend on the rest of the world. They adopted their own culture. And to be honest, people where living on a small island without any sense of direction. Young people had nothing to do. There was no perspective about what to do with their lifes. They have their own legal systems, their own culture and politics. I think this was a perfect example of what a human mind could be. And it's rather unattractive. It's an island economy and there is no development. No resources and days come and go.

Escapism in Puerto Viejo

I met Tracy, Cuni and a bunch of young people in puerto viejo. Most of them felt comfortable with the slow pace of the town. There are many festivals and only tourists come to this town. They have enough food, opportunities to get to know foreigners and to learn English. They had strong political opinions. For example: they were protesting for freedom of palastine, women rights and stopping world's hunger. But yet, it seemed that they also tried to met their own desires. They wanted a place to meet each other and to protect themselves in times of personal development. In that sense it was a strong bubble. They mostly didn't have children and took only responsibility for themselves. It was more like a place to heal. Some of them were much older. Some of them were also fighting with staying or going back to the capital. They knew that staying wouldn't bring new development, since the community maintained the status quo. The status quo is definitely not something bad. I had the feeling that their desires and their actions were not aligned. Most of the time I found them playing games on their smartphones and they felt bored. They worked for big hostals owned by Spanish or American folks. That means that Ticos were not enriching themselves, but the rich foreigners were getting the money. It is a mutual beneficial deal, but at the end the Ticos cut short and become the losers. This was obvious. And they knew it. The Ticos knew that were not invited to induce change. The political actions of the Ticos were tolerated, since the effort required to shut down the actions were too big. I found the same kind of escapism in other hostals across central america. Sometimes people were convinced that it was only a current stage of their lifes. For others, it was again a place to heal and to process their depressions. In Sámara, the hostal stuff was smoking weed all the time and they gave a really bad service. They intended to to do the minimal and take time to rest. They also didn't really cared about their jobs, because they were also aware that the system was quite amoral. Their work place exploited them, because they were paid little. To fight this injustice, they would just cheat at work. For example, they worked with smartphones in hand, didn't gave customer service.
